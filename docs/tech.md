# Tech

Three.js r170 (vendored), plain ES modules, no build step, and no asset files but sounds (`sounds/`): `lili-growl-*.wav` are Lili's own play growl, the sound she makes pulling on something, recorded by the owner and kept for pulling a toy from someone; the rest (bark, panting, car engine, horn and crash, a man's yell, a woman's and a child's scream) are generated (*Generated sounds* below), the lines said out loud (`sounds/voice/*.mp3`) are made by a speech model (*The voice* below), and everything else is synthesised in `src/audio.js`. Same architecture as wheek.

## The town: tiles on a map

Built from the design in `docs/open-world-design.md` (steps 1 to 4 done).

- **The map**, `world/map.txt`: a legend (`R = road`: a letter and the tile file it stands for), `bounds x0 y0 x1 y1` (how far she can go; the hedge runs round it), then the grid, one cell per tile, separated by spaces, north at the top. A cell may be turned: `>` a quarter turn clockwise, `>>` half, `<` a quarter anticlockwise. Read by `loadTown()` in `src/world.js` (the game waits for it before starting).
- **A tile**, `world/tiles/<name>.js`: an ES module whose default export says what is on a 400 × 400 square (`TILE`), in its own coordinates (x east, y south, 0 to 400), before it is turned:
  - `ground`: shapes, painted in this order: `{ path: [x0, y0, x1, y1] }` (sand, `PATH_HALF` 42 each side), `{ plaza: [x, y, r] }`, `{ road: [x0, y0, x1, y1] }` or `{ road: { arc: [cx, cy, r, a0, a1] } }` (asphalt `ROAD_HALF` 110 each side of the line, then `WALK` 70 of pavement), `{ zebra: [x, y] }` (across a west-east road), `{ bay: [x, y, w, h] }` (a parking bay's lines), `{ sand: [x, y, r] }` (a sandpit with a wooden rim), `{ pave: [x0, y0, x1, y1] }` (paving, as a town square; counts as pavement), `{ water: [x0, y0, x1, y1, r] }` and `{ beach: [x0, y0, x1, y1, r] }` (rounded rectangles, which may reach far beyond the tile: water, painted as a plain lake bed under the water's surface, and sand round it). A road reaching the middle of the tile from an edge (as in `cross` and `tee`) gets a whole number of dashes too, so the middle lines stop short of a junction. The same shapes say what is underfoot (`tileSurface`, and `world.surfaceAt(x, y)` in town coordinates).
  - things: `lamps: [[x, y]]`, `trees: [[x, y]]`, `benches: [[x, y]]` (west-east), `table: [x, y]` (with four plates), `house: [x, y]`, `home: [x, y]` (Lili's), `school: [x, y]`, `shop: [kind, x, y]` (`butcher`, `bakery` or `cafe`: what they sell shown in pictures in the window and on the sign, a striped awning; colours in `SHOP`), `fixtures: [[kind, x, y]]` (solid things that stay put, each a model of its kind, `fixtureGeometry`: `kennel` with her bowl, `fountain`, `cafe-table`; sizes in `FIXTURE_R`), `stalls: [[x, y]]` (market stalls, west-east counters 58 high she can jump onto), `props: [[kind, x, y, z?]]` (kinds in `KINDS`), `birds: [[x, y]]` (a flock), `flowers: n` (scattered on the tile's grass), `park: true` (scared birds may come down here), `spots: [[tag, x, y]]` (named places for missions; `start` is where she begins).
  - Everything on a tile is turned with it, and a tile used in several cells puts its things in each.
- **People and the car** are not part of tiles: `world/people.js` places them on the map, so any tile can be used any number of times. `at: [col, row, x, y]` is a map square (counting from 0, top left) and a place in it (0 to 400 across and down, not turned with the tile); `walk: [[col, row, x, y], [col, row, x, y]]` goes to and fro between two places, across squares if need be; `facing: north | south | east | west`; `look` and `nature` as in *People* below. The car is `car: { at, facing }`.
- **No seams**: grass patches are placed by their position in town (`patches` in `src/ground.js`), so they run across tile edges; a road's dashed line is a whole number of dash lengths per tile, with half a gap at each end, so it runs on whichever way neighbouring tiles are turned. A road reaches a tile's edge at its middle, so roads meet.
- **The kit** (26 tiles): `road`, `corner`, `tee`, `cross`, `crossing`, `parking`, `parcels`, `roadworks`; `grass`, `house`, `home`, `school`; `lawn`, `trees`, `path`, `plaza`, `benches`, `pots`, `picnic`, `playground`; the town centre: `butcher`, `bakery`, `cafe`, `fountain`, `square`, `market` (stalls with apples, a prop kind that rolls; resting things up high must sit exactly on a top, or they fall); the lake: `lake`, `shore`, `shore-corner`, `beach` (the shore with a ball and ducks).
- **The town** (2026-11-15) is 17 × 13 tiles (6800 × 5200): a ring road with a middle road and two cross streets, six blocks inside (houses round lawns, the park with the plaza, the playground, the town centre: three shops facing south onto a paved square with a fountain, benches and market stalls; the lake, 3 × 3 tiles with a sandy beach all round), houses all round outside. Lili's home and the school are on the north side of the ring, facing the road: the camera looks north, so a building north of a road shows its front and one south of it its back.
- **Buildings** (houses, Lili's home, the school) face south on their tile (front door that way) and turn with it (`dir`). They are solid: a row of circles along each (`kind: 'house'` obstacles), so she bonks into them at a gallop. Benches and stalls turn with their tile too (`dir`). A building between the camera and her fades to see-through, like trees (it stood in the way of the road beyond it).
- **Only what is near is built** (`streamTiles` in `src/scene.js`): each tile keeps what stands on it and does not move (`t.things`: trees, lamps, benches and the table, buildings, kennels); within 3 tiles to either side, 4 ahead (north, where the camera looks) and 2 behind, times the camera's zoom, its ground is painted and its things built, nearest first, two tiles a frame; beyond 2 more it is dropped (meshes removed, textures and materials freed). Painting a tile takes 1 to 2 ms. People, birds, props, flowers, the car and the hedge are always there. The ground beyond what is built is plain grass, which the fog mostly hides.

## Missions (`src/missions.js`, `missions/`)

The design is *Jobs for five to seven year olds* and *Missions: the files and the runner* in `docs/open-world-design.md`; a "job" there is a mission here. A mission is a markdown file in `missions/`, listed in `missions/list.txt` (the order they are offered in); its header (a small subset of YAML, read by `parseMission`) gives `id`, `level` (1 to 4), `needs` (optional), `picture` (a list of icon names from `ICONS` in `src/art.js`), `say`, `praise` (optional), `careful` (optional: an event that empties its progress) and `steps`; notes for us go below it. A step is one action with a tag as its target, and optionally `count`, `say`, `picture`, `from`, `to`, `at`. The actions are listed in the design doc. Spots are the tiles' `spots` tags (`table`, `school-door`, `home`, `beach`, `lake`, `shore`, ...); "there" is within `NEAR` (drive 350, so the road in front of a building counts; go 120; poo 110; bring 130).

The runner (`makeRunner`) holds one job at a time: its step, how many times the step is done (`progress()`), the things already counted (`seen`: the same pot, person or bone counts once), and which jobs are done. `LEVEL_AT` is the number of stickers that opens each level (0, 4, 9, 15). The game tells it what happens (`missions.event(type, { kind, x, y, id, from })`); it checks `go` and `drive` itself each frame (`missions.update`). `missions.wants(action)` is the step in hand if it is that action, `missions.near(x, y)` whether that is the step's place: for what the game offers to do (a poo, dropping what she carries). `missions.target(dog, car)` is where the way should lead. `missions.next()` is the next job to offer: the first one open and not done after the last one in hand, in list order. It calls back through hooks (`start`, `step`, `progress`, `oops`, `done`). Nothing in Lili's or people's code knows about missions.

Events the game sends (`src/main.js`): `zoom`, `jump`, `bark`, `honk`, `chase` (a flock), `smash` and `knock` (the kind of prop; `lamp` from the car), `hop` (the kind of platform she lands on), `swim`, `splash` (a jump into water), `shake` (out of the water), `greet`, `scare` (child or adult), `take`, `pick`, `bring`, `poo`, `collect` (a bone), `dig`.

What the game does for them (`src/main.js`, section "jobs" and around it):
- **The card** (`drawJob`): top middle, or under the counters when the screen is too narrow (`cardBox`). New, it is drawn big in the middle for 2.4 s and eases to its place. Pictures from the step in hand if it has its own, else the job's; pips for a count, or one per step. The words (`ui.say`) are drawn under it while it is new or just tapped. Tapping it (`input.onTap`) says it again and sets `ui.helpT` (14 s of guidance).
- **Said out loud** (`A.speak(text)`): a clip per line (*The voice* below), found by its words in `sounds/voice/lines.json`, fetched and decoded the first time it is said. One line at a time (a new one cuts the last short); it waits for the first touch (browsers allow no sound before one) and stops when muted. A line with no clip is not said: there is no other voice to fall back on.
- **Finding the way** (`guided`, `drawTrail`): on for levels 1 and 2, and otherwise while `ui.helpT` runs or after 25 s with no progress (`missions.quiet()`). White paw prints with a shadow from her (or the car) towards `missions.target`, lighting up one after another; within 180 a bouncing marker; out of sight, a green arrow at the edge of the screen. The bark or jump button pulses (class `hint`) when the step wants it.
- **Done** (`jobDone`, `drawCelebration`, `updateJobs`): fanfare, confetti, praise; the sticker grows in the middle, then flies to the book button (2.6 s); if a level opened, a banner with its stars; the next job starts 3.6 s after (6.4 s with a banner). None left: free play.
- **The sticker book** (`#btn-book`, `drawBook`, `tapBook`): drawn on the overlay; while it is open `frame` skips `update` (the town waits) and the action buttons are hidden. `bookLayout` picks the number of columns that gives the biggest stickers for the screen. Tapping a sticker of an open level starts that job; a locked one says how many stickers are missing. Escape or the button closes it.
- **Saving** (`save`, `load`): `localStorage['lili-save-1']` = `{ v, done, job, last, bones, mess, birds }`, written when a job starts or ends, when a bone is found and every 10 s. `?fresh` clears it.
- **Poo**: when the step wants a poo where she has stopped (standing still 0.8 s; up on the thing if it is one to be on, `onIt`), she squats (`dog.poo`, 1.6 s) and leaves a poo there.
- **Toys**: a person can hold a prop (`holds: 'teddy'` or `'sausage'` in `world/people.js`; the prop remembers its `owner`). Trotting into them she grabs it, and it is a tug of war won by tapping (`dog.tug`, the person's `tug` state): every tap on the screen, button or key is a yank (`yank`: `TUG_YANK` 0.085 of the bar, a jerk back, dust, her play growl at most once a second), and they pull back all the time (`TUG_BACK` 0.09 a second) from `TUG_START` 0.4. Her side full, they let go and sit down with a bump and it is in her mouth (`dog.carry`); theirs full, she lets go and they keep it. About three taps a second wins in four seconds; no taps, they win in four and a half. The pull runs across the screen (`tug.nx`: she goes round to their side she came from), because along the camera's view her head hid the toy; her mouth is a toy's length from their hands, and the toy lies along the pull, stretched (`toy.tugged`, `toy.stretch`; `TUG` in `src/scene.js`). The tug bar over them (`drawTug`): her face at her end, theirs at theirs, the toy where the pulling is, her side pink; and the buttons pulse. With or without a job. A carriable thing lying loose (`KINDS.carry`) is picked up when she runs over it.
- **Playing with people** (`updatePlay`): with a toy (`TOY`: the teddy) in her mouth, standing still for 0.5 s beside a child or a dog lover with empty hands, they take it (state `play`): 60% throw it over her head (300 to 420 a second, up 560) for her to fetch, otherwise they hold it out for a tug (and keep it after 8 s). Not while a job wants the toy brought somewhere. A toy someone else holds goes back to its owner when a job needs it (`giveBack`).
- **Pick-up pause**: a thing put down or knocked has a short `cool` before she can pick it up or knock it again; it counts down at rest too (it used to stop when the thing settled, and she could never pick up again what she had put down).
- **In her mouth** (`carryAt`): what she carries hangs from a point between her front teeth on the dog model (`bite`, on the jaw) and swings like a pendulum 40 long: pushed by the mouth speeding up, slowing and turning (from its position frame to frame), jiggled by her stride, damped, at most 1 rad (`swingF` forward and back, `swingS` side to side). `src/scene.js` hangs it from the real mouth with those (`HANG`: the teddy by its head, the sausages by one end).
- **Putting down**: the purple button (shown only while she carries something; G or C; pad B) puts it down where she is; so does standing still where a `bring` step wants it, or a bark. Put down within 110 of the basket by her kennel, it goes in the basket (`intoBasket`) and stays there (`o.kept`: not tidied or given back) until she runs at it to take it out, or a job needs it back with its owner. `home` for a `bring` step is the basket.
- **Bones** (`updateFinds`, `gotBone`): props of a kind with `find` (bone) are picked up when she touches them, on foot or in the car; a bone flies to the counter (`G.bones`). Gone for 60 s and until she is 1200 away. In the 3D view they hover and turn.
- **Digging** (in `updateDog`): standing still within 62 of a `mound` (kind with `dig`) for 0.3 s she digs (the play-bow pose, earth flying, `A.dig`), and after 1.7 s the heap is gone and a bone is hers (`dig` then `collect`). Heaps come back like bones, after 90 s.
- **Putting back** (`tidy`, `tidyFar`, `prepare`, `giveBack`): when a job starts, each step gets what it needs: too few pots or cones left and all of that kind are back at `home`; a `take` step returns the thing to its owner; lamp posts stand up again. Every 2 s, things broken or knocked over more than 1500 away are put back.

The 26 jobs are in `missions/`; the levels and what each teaches are in the design doc.

## The car knocks down light poles

A light pole hit by the car at more than 150 (`updateCar`) goes over: it stops being solid (taken out of `world.obstacles`), tips over its base away from the car, faster and faster, in 0.55 s, bounces once on the ground and stays lying there (`o.fallen`, drawn in `render`); a crash (sound, the car shudders), stars, one on the mess counter, and the car stops dead and rocks back at 45, so the pole falls in front of it (it ploughed on at 0.75 at first, too light; then kept 0.2 and rolled onto the fallen pole, which showed through the car). Lili running into a pole only shakes it. Fallen poles do not stand up again (yet).

## Swimming

On water (`world.surfaceAt` is `'water'`) she swims (`dog.swim`, in `updateDog`): at `SWIM` 0.5 of her speed, never in zoomies; sunk 30 and bobbing, tilted chest-up so her rump is low, head up, legs stepping out of sight under the ground (which is the water's surface), a slow wag. Her waterline is the lake's surface, and she makes her own ripples and wake by pushing the water (*Water* below; `updateSwim` in `src/scene.js`): her chest pushes it up and down in rhythm (a steady push piled it into a mound), gently, a little harder the faster she goes. She casts no shadow while swimming (on the water it made her look as if she lay on top of it). Before the ripple simulation, rings and a V of white streaks were drawn round her by hand; they went wrong twice (turned the wrong way, then switched off by a misplaced comment). Leaping into the lake (`land()` on water) is a big splash, made the way cartoon games make one: a crown (`crown` in `src/scene.js`: one sheet of water, an open ring flaring outward as it rises with its rim drawn up into uneven points, that shoots up round her and falls back as it spreads, 0.8 s; separate rounded tongues looked like bowling pins), a handful of chunky drops (the `drop` particle: pale blue with a white highlight, its edge just softened, shrinking as they fall), a little mist at its foot (`mist`), `A.splash`, and a push of 2.5 on the water (a ring). Tried first: small outlined drops (too defined), then many soft puffs (too foggy). Jump in the water is a smaller splash instead (`jump()`): 8 drops and a little mist, `A.splash`, a push of 1.6 on the water, and she surges on 120; at most every 0.5 s. Climbing out, she shakes at once (`dog.shakeWet`, 1.4 s), and keeps shaking while she walks slowly on if steered (her speed held to `SHAKE_WALK` 0.35 of a trot, no zoomies; steering does not cancel this shake as it does others), and soft spray (the `mist` particle) is flung off her coat in bursts, to one side and then the other with the shake's swing (separate drops were too literal). While swimming and while shaking she ignores birds (no look, yip or thought bubble). Before, she only shook once she stood still on her own. The car cannot go in: its ends stop at the water's edge as at a wall.

## Water (`src/water.js`)

The lake's surface is one see-through sheet over all the water in town, `WATER_Y` 12 above the ground, drawn by a shader. When the town is built, a distance map is measured over the water (a 2-pass chamfer on a grid of `CELL` 8, up to 255 from the shore, as a texture), so the shading is smooth across tiles whatever shape they make. The shader draws in the game's flat cartoon style (a first version with smooth shading, noisy wavelets and sparkles was, in the owner's words, hyper-realistic in a cartoon world): three flat colour bands by distance from the shore (45 and 130), a few short white arcs drifting slowly (one at most per 90-unit cell), a solid white band of foam along the shore whose edge waves gently, and thin white lines where the ripples crest. Under it the ground is a plain lake bed. What is lower than the surface shows through tinted, which is Lili's waterline when she swims.

**Ripples**: the water's height is simulated on the GPU (`makeRipples`): a grid of `RIPPLE_CELL` 6 over the lake, two half-float render targets taken in turn, a wave step 30 times a second (each cell: its neighbours' heights halved, less its own before; damped `DAMP` 0.94, so they die out within a body length or two of her: the owner asked for them gentle, slow and just round her; held flat within 3 of the shore, so waves bounce off it). `water.push(x, y, r, s)` adds a bump (a Gaussian, `PUSHES` 4 a step at most). The surface shader draws a thin white line round each crest (heights 0.09 to 0.17). Waves travel about 125 units a second. Needs WebGL 2 with float or half-float render targets; without them the lake has no ripples and nothing else changes. Replaced (2026-11-15): water painted into the ground in bands, with a see-through disc round her as a waterline.

## Coordinates and camera

Logic is on a flat map: `x` east, `y` south, `z` height. In the scene, map `y` is three.js `z` and height is three.js `y`. Models face +x; `root.rotation.y = -heading`.

The camera looks north from above and behind, never turns, leads the dog by 0.3 s of his velocity and backs off at speed and in the car (`G.focus.zoom`). On a narrow screen it backs off further (`fit`). Leaves between the camera and the dog, and trees in the near foreground, fade.

## How the dog moves (`updateDog`)

Constants at the top of `src/main.js`: `TROT` 210, `ZOOM` 460 (2.5 and 5.5 m/s at 84 units to the metre: a spaniel's trot, and the simulated gallop's own speed; they were 420 and 640 until 2026-11-14, which looked frantic, then 210 and 320, which looked slow at full speed; after `ZOOM_AFTER` 0.9 s flat out), `JUMP_V` 430, `GRAV` 1900 (a leap about 45 high and 0.45 s long), `JUMP_UP` 560 (about 82 high, used when there is something to get up onto ahead), `BRAKE` 2.3 × `ZOOM` and `SKID_END` 0.23 × `ZOOM` (the about-turn slides about a third of a second from flat out; with fixed values the slide vanished when the speeds were halved), `BONK` 0.73 × `ZOOM` (speed into a solid thing that dazes him), `CAR_TOP` 900.

- On the ground, velocity eases toward steering × top speed: fast at a trot, slowly in zoomies (that is the skid).
- In the air it eases toward steering at no less than 0.75 × `TROT`.
- Solid things are circles (`world.obstacles`) and capsules with a flat top (`world.platforms`: benches, table, the parked car). A platform is solid from the side and floor from above (`floorAt`).
- States: `idle`, `paws`, `daze`, `hop` (scripted arc into and out of the car), `drive`. The model reads the state and velocity and blends its own pose (`makeDog().update`).

## How the dog moves (`makeDog` in `src/models.js`)

On the ground nothing is recorded or key-framed; only a jump plays motion data (`src/moves.js`, below). The legs are solved every frame:

- **Paws stand on the ground.** Each paw has a place in map coordinates. While it stands it does not move; the leg is bent to reach from the joint to the paw. Each leg has three bones in the proportions of the simulated skeleton, scaled to her leg height (`BONES` in `src/moves.js`): upper arm, forearm, pastern; thigh, shin and the bone below the hock. Three bones can reach a paw in many ways, so `solve()` sets the lowest by rule (it leans with the leg, the pastern folds back as a forepaw lifts, the hock sinks as a hind leg folds) and the other two then have one answer (elbow back, knee forward). A paw that cannot be reached any more lifts.
- **The stride follows the ground.** The gait cycle advances with the distance the paws have to cover (the average speed of the four rest positions, so turning on the spot counts), never with the clock. When a paw's turn comes it swings to a spot ahead of its shoulder: half of what the shoulder will cover while the paw stands. Trot is diagonal pairs; gallop is hind pair, front pair, then all four off the ground. Faster means a longer stride and a shorter share of it on the ground.
- **Standing still**, any paw left more than a few units from its rest position steps back under the body.
- **Poses** (jump, hello, bow, point, daze, driving) hold a paw at an angle and length from its joint instead; each paw blends between the pose and its place on the ground.
- **Body skin.** Chest, back, belly and rump are one surface (`torsoGeo`), not separate shapes. Each frame `bendBody` moves its vertices: the rump end with the rear half of the spine, the chest end with the front half, blended between. Tail likewise has one surface (`TAIL`); neck, head, ears and legs are still ellipsoids.
- **Turning.** In `updateDog` the heading turns towards the stick at a limited rate (8.5 rad/s trotting, 5.5 galloping, 4 in the air) and the velocity follows the heading, so a change of direction is a curve; trotting, speed drops with the angle still to turn, so an about-turn is walked round almost on the spot. The model reads the turn rate: the spine bends in the middle, the head and tail lead into the turn, and the body leans in at speed.
- **Jump.** `jump()` does not lift her: it sets `dog.spring` and decides `dog.high` (a bench, the table or the car within about half a second ahead of where she will leave the ground). From a stand (`dog.still`) she stays where she is for 0.34 s and plays the start of a captured move: she sinks, rocks back and drives off her hind legs. From a run the run-up is 0.3 s down to 0.12 s at a trot or faster, straight on at full strength whatever the pointer says, with a posed gather over the last 0.11 s. `takeOff()` launches her at the speed she has (at least 240) with `JUMP_V`, or `JUMP_UP` when `dog.high`. **A jump at something lands on it** (`jumpAim`, `dog.aim`): if a bench, the table, a market stall or the car is within `AIM_FAR` 260 and within about 50° of the way she faces (90° when she is right beside it, and any way she faces if she is standing beside it: standing, she turns to point at birds by herself), she turns to the part of it she is heading for over the run-up (from a stand further than 150 away she takes a 0.3 s run at it; at its foot she goes up at once), leaves the ground straight at it at the speed that brings her down on its middle (at most `AIM_SPEED` 540), is not steered, nor stopped by the side of anything to stand on, on the way, and on landing stops there for 0.6 s (`dog.stay`), out of the zoomies and with the tapped target forgotten, so that she does not run off the far side. In the air she plays the flight of the capture (`stand` or `high` in `src/moves.js`): each frame gives her tilt, the bend of her back and, for each leg, the angles of its three bones against her body. The flight is stretched to the time she is really in the air (`dog.flight`, and `dog.eta`, the time left until she is down), so landing on a bench still ends on the landing frame. The ground stops any paw. Off a run her tilt is scaled to 0.7. A fall off an edge starts the capture from its top. After landing the legs blend back to the ground. `python tools/sim/export.py` writes `src/moves.js` from `tools/sim/out/`. The hop into the car is still posed by hand. The ears take 70% of her rise and fall with them and are flapped a fifth as much as on the ground, so they hang and trail instead of rising like wings.

## Music (`src/music.js`)

A bouncy tune for walking round town, written as a score in the file and played by synthesised instruments, so there is no music file: 32 bars in C at 108 beats a minute (about 70 s, A A' B A) round and round. A toy marimba (sine with a little of its octave and double octave) carries the tune, a soft triangle pluck plays the chord on each off-beat, a round bass plays the root on the first beat and the fifth below on the third, and a high-passed noise shaker ticks the eighths. Notes are scheduled a quarter of a second ahead from a 50 ms timer, from the audio clock, so a slow frame does not make it stumble; a hidden tab suspends the audio and it picks up from where the clock is.

- It starts on the title's play (the first touch is what allows sound), fades in over a couple of seconds, and goes under the mute button with everything else.
- Level: `LOUD` 0.2 on the music's own gain, which renders at about −33 dB RMS against about −17 dB for a spoken line and −18 dB for the bark (measured with `__G.api.renderMusic()`, which renders the whole tune offline). While a line is said it dips to 35% of that (`duck`), and comes back after it.
- To change the tune, edit the bars (`A`, `A2`, `B`: a note per eighth, `-` holds, `.` rests) and the chords (`CA`, `CB`, `NOTES`).

## The simulated skeleton (`tools/sim/`)

Movement posed by hand did not look like a dog. `tools/sim/dog.py` is a springer spaniel's skeleton for MuJoCo, side on: back, neck, head, tail, and for each leg the real bones (shoulder blade, upper arm, forearm, wrist to paw; thigh, shin, hock to paw), with a dog's joint ranges, about 20 kg spread over the bones, and servos limited to a dog's strength. `tools/sim/jump.py` drives the joints from knots over time and searches (cross-entropy, 16 processes, about 3 minutes) for the leap that reaches the asked height and speed, lands forepaws first, ends standing and costs the least effort. The first guess is rough; the result is whatever the physics allows.

- Each case in `CASES` says what is asked: take-off speed, how far her middle rises, and for the high jump how far she must sink first (`crouch`, 13 cm) and how far she may rear (`tip`). Without `crouch` the search found a high jump with almost no wind-up, which is not how a dog does it.
- `python jump.py stand` (long leap) or `high`; `python jump.py stand 0` replays the saved result. Setup, once, outside the repo: `python3 -m venv ~/sim-venv && ~/sim-venv/bin/pip install mujoco numpy pillow`, then run the tools from `tools/sim` with `~/sim-venv/bin/python` (made with MuJoCo 4.0.1 on Python 3.14).
- Output in `tools/sim/out/`: `<case>.json` (every joint's position 100 times a second, and which paws are down), `<case>.png` (a strip), `<case>.npy` (the knots, to resume a search).
- `tools/sim/view.html` plays a capture in slow motion: `/tools/sim/view.html` on the local server.
- **Gaits.** `tools/sim/gait.py` searches for a steady trot or gallop: `python gait.py trot` or `gallop` (about 5 minutes; a second run resumes from the saved result, `--fresh` starts over, `0` replays). Each joint's target is a repeating curve over the stride (a mean and three harmonics), and the four legs run the same curve at different points of the stride (`lag` in `CASES`: diagonal pairs together for the trot; for the gallop the search may move them). She starts standing at the asked speed and runs 7 strides; the first 3 do not count. The cost asks for: the asked speed, no fall, each stride like the last, her middle kept at standing height and level, each paw on the ground for a share of the stride within `duty`, nothing but paws touching the ground, least effort. One stride (the sixth) is recorded to `out/<case>.json` with `loop: true`. `python export.py` writes them into `src/moves.js` as `GAITS`, measured against the standing skeleton (the first frame of `out/stand.json`), with each paw's time on the ground read from its contacts. The skeleton is flat, so left and right are only names: the trot found had the fore and hind paw of the same name down together, which would be a pace, so the trot case swaps the hind legs' names when it records (`swap`).
  - Results so far (`tools/sim/out/`, play them in `tools/sim/view.html`): `trot` 2.4 m/s, stride 0.36 s (86 cm), diagonal pairs, each paw down 25 to 30% of the stride, her middle rising and falling 0.8 cm (standard deviation): `gait.py trot 150 --bounce=0.008` from `trot_bouncy`, because in the game `trot_bouncy` read as too jumpy. `trot_bouncy` (1.5 cm, `bounce=0.015`) was the one approved in the viewer. `trot_flat` is the first trot (stride 0.30 s, paws down 35%, her middle held level): judged not jumpy enough. The bouncy one came from adding `bounce=0.015` (the cost then asks for that much rise and fall instead of none), `Tmin=0.36` and a lower `duty` range to the trot case, and running `gait.py trot 150` from the flat one. `gallop` 3.8 m/s (her `ZOOM`), stride 0.32 s (1.2 m), clean contacts: `gait.py gallop 150 --v=3.8` from `gallop_fast`. `gallop_fast` 5.5 m/s, stride 0.32 s (1.75 m), a moment in the air; a hock still brushes the ground now and then (9 contacts in 4 strides); it was the game's gallop while her top speed was 640. `gallop_quick` 5.5 m/s, stride 0.25 s, clean contacts, but four strides a second is quicker than a spaniel's.
  - How the gallop was reached: searched directly at 5.5 m/s from a hand guess it never stood up (pitched, scrambling at 3.3 m/s). What worked was going in steps from the trot: `gait.py gallop 150 --from=trot --v=3.0` (the trot's curves with the gallop's leg timing), then `gait.py gallop 120 --v=4.2`, then `gait.py gallop 120 --v=5.5`, each resuming from the one before. That gave `gallop_quick` (the stride sat at the 0.25 s lower limit). `Tmin=0.32` was then added to the gallop case and `gait.py gallop 150` run once more for the longer stride.
  - What went wrong first, and why the cost is as it is: with only speed, repetition and effort asked, the trot came out crouched with the hind paws barely touching (on the ground 11% of the stride). Hence the height term and the `duty` range.
