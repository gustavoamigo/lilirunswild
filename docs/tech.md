# Tech

Three.js r170 (vendored), plain ES modules, no build step, and no asset files but sounds (`sounds/CREDITS.md`): the bark is a soundfont sample, because a synthesised bark did not sound like a dog, and `sounds/lili-growl-*.wav` are Lili's own play growl, the sound she makes pulling on something. It is kept for pulling a toy from someone and plays nowhere until that exists (not with the play bow). Same architecture as wheek.

## Coordinates and camera

Logic is on a flat map: `x` east, `y` south, `z` height. In the scene, map `y` is three.js `z` and height is three.js `y`. Models face +x; `root.rotation.y = -heading`.

The camera looks north from above and behind, never turns, leads the dog by 0.3 s of his velocity and backs off at speed and in the car (`G.focus.zoom`). On a narrow screen it backs off further (`fit`). Leaves between the camera and the dog, and trees in the near foreground, fade.

## How the dog moves (`updateDog`)

Constants at the top of `src/main.js`: `TROT` 210, `ZOOM` 460 (2.5 and 5.5 m/s at 84 units to the metre: a spaniel's trot, and the simulated gallop's own speed; they were 420 and 640 until 2026-11-14, which looked frantic, then 210 and 320, which looked slow at full speed; after `ZOOM_AFTER` 0.9 s flat out), `JUMP_V` 430, `GRAV` 1900 (a leap about 45 high and 0.45 s long), `JUMP_UP` 560 (about 82 high, used when there is something to get up onto ahead), `BRAKE` 2.3 × `ZOOM` and `SKID_END` 0.23 × `ZOOM` (the about-turn slides about a third of a second from flat out; with fixed values the slide vanished when the speeds were halved), `BONK` 0.73 × `ZOOM` (speed into a solid thing that dazes him), `CAR_TOP` 900.

- On the ground, velocity eases toward steering × top speed: fast at a trot, slowly in zoomies (that is the skid).
- In the air it eases toward steering at no less than 0.75 × `TROT`.
- Solid things are circles (`world.obstacles`) and capsules with a flat top (`world.platforms`: benches, table, the parked car). A platform is solid from the side and floor from above (`floorAt`).
- States: `idle`, `paws`, `daze`, `hop` (scripted arc into and out of the car), `drive`. The model reads the state and velocity and blends its own pose (`makeDog().update`).

## How the dog moves (`makeDog` in `src/models.js`)

On the ground nothing is recorded or key-framed; only a jump plays motion data (`src/moves.js`, below). The legs are solved every frame:

- **Paws stand on the ground.** Each paw has a place in map coordinates. While it stands it does not move; the leg is bent to reach from the joint to the paw. Each leg has three bones in the proportions of the simulated skeleton, scaled to her leg height (`BONES` in `src/moves.js`): upper arm, forearm, pastern; thigh, shin and the bone below the hock. Three bones can reach a paw in many ways, so `solve()` sets the lowest by rule (it leans with the leg, the pastern folds back as a forepaw lifts, the hock sinks as a hind leg folds) and the other two then have one answer (elbow back, knee forward). A paw that cannot be reached any more lifts.
- **The stride follows the ground.** The gait cycle advances with the distance the paws have to cover (the average speed of the four rest positions, so turning on the spot counts), never with the clock. When a paw's turn comes it swings to a spot ahead of its shoulder: half of what the shoulder will cover while the paw stands. Trot is diagonal pairs; gallop is hind pair, front pair, then all four off the ground. Faster means a longer stride and a shorter share of it on the ground.
- **Standing still**, any paw left more than a few units from its rest position steps back under the body.
- **Poses** (jump, hello, bow, point, daze, driving) hold a paw at an angle and length from its joint instead; each paw blends between the pose and its place on the ground.
- **Body skin.** Chest, back, belly and rump are one surface (`torsoGeo`), not separate shapes. Each frame `bendBody` moves its vertices: the rump end with the rear half of the spine, the chest end with the front half, blended between. Tail likewise has one surface (`TAIL`); neck, head, ears and legs are still ellipsoids.
- **Turning.** In `updateDog` the heading turns towards the stick at a limited rate (8.5 rad/s trotting, 5.5 galloping, 4 in the air) and the velocity follows the heading, so a change of direction is a curve; trotting, speed drops with the angle still to turn, so an about-turn is walked round almost on the spot. The model reads the turn rate: the spine bends in the middle, the head and tail lead into the turn, and the body leans in at speed.
- **Jump.** `jump()` does not lift her: it sets `dog.spring` and decides `dog.high` (a bench, the table or the car within about half a second ahead of where she will leave the ground). From a stand (`dog.still`) she stays where she is for 0.34 s and plays the start of a captured move: she sinks, rocks back and drives off her hind legs. From a run the run-up is 0.3 s down to 0.12 s at a trot or faster, straight on at full strength whatever the pointer says, with a posed gather over the last 0.11 s. `takeOff()` launches her at the speed she has (at least 240) with `JUMP_V`, or `JUMP_UP` when `dog.high`. In the air she plays the flight of the capture (`stand` or `high` in `src/moves.js`): each frame gives her tilt, the bend of her back and, for each leg, the angles of its three bones against her body. The flight is stretched to the time she is really in the air (`dog.flight`, and `dog.eta`, the time left until she is down), so landing on a bench still ends on the landing frame. The ground stops any paw. Off a run her tilt is scaled to 0.7. A fall off an edge starts the capture from its top. After landing the legs blend back to the ground. `python tools/sim/export.py` writes `src/moves.js` from `tools/sim/out/`. The hop into the car is still posed by hand. The ears take 70% of her rise and fall with them and are flapped a fifth as much as on the ground, so they hang and trail instead of rising like wings.
- **About-turn at a gallop.** Asking for a direction more than about 115° off her travel while galloping (faster than 330) sets `dog.skid`: she brakes at `BRAKE` 1500 with the front legs braced and the haunches low. Below 150 the brake hands over to `dog.push`: she is back at a trot and steps round as in any trot turn, and once she faces within 0.45 rad of where she is going she pushes off at 330, straight into the gallop. Letting go of the stick, a jump or a bump ends it. With the pointer, steering strength falls with the pointer's distance from her, so a pointer dragged across her to the other side used to read as "slow down" and she had lost the gallop before it was behind her. While the pointer is moving on screen (more than 350 px/s, and for 0.3 s after) a galloping dog ignores its distance and keeps going flat out, also while it is right on top of her, so the reversal meets her at speed. A pointer held still is unchanged: she slows as she reaches it. After the turn she pushes off into a gallop only if the pointer is far enough to ask for one.
- **Running.** Walk and trot are hand-made (the rules in `update` in `src/models.js`): paws planted on spots, a cycle driven by the ground covered (a stride of 16 to 28 for each paw's reach), diagonal pairs at the trot, the body's bob by formula. Flat out (zoomies, from 260 up, fully by 340; `gw` is how much) she plays the simulated gallop (`GAITS.gallop` in `src/moves.js`, from `tools/sim/out/gallop_fast`, 5.5 m/s) whole: each leg's bones at the simulated angles, so the paws go where the simulated paws went (only the ground stops them; they are not pinned to spots while she gallops); the body's tilt, bend and rise and fall; the tail (the simulated tail hangs from a soft spring with nothing driving it, so its swing is what the run does to it). The neck (swung about its root at the collar) and the head keep their simulated average carriage but only `HEAD_KEEP` 0.35 of the swing: at full swing her head bobbed heavily. The stride covers its true length (147) and the tempo follows the ground she covers, within 0.75 to 1.5 of the simulated tempo (`TEMPO`); `ZOOM` is the gallop's own speed, so flat out it plays at its own tempo. Footfall sounds come from its touchdowns. The brakes, jumps and every pose still take her out of it. History: from 2026-11-13 to 11-14 she ran the simulated trot as well, in part and then whole; on her rounded, toy-like body the trot read as uncanny and was taken out, while the owner judged the simulated gallop good and asked to have it back.
- **Spine.** The chest and the haunches are two halves hinged in the middle. At a gallop they fold under as the hind paws reach forward and open out as she stretches; the leg joints move with them.
- **Ears.** Each ear is a chain of four points hanging from the head, simulated every frame (gravity, drag, a weak spring towards hanging straight, kept outside the head). The air she moves through lifts them out sideways and flutters them, more the faster she goes, and the head rolls a little with the stride, so they flap in and out and up and down as well as trailing (slowly: about 1 to 2 flaps a second). The wind of her own speed pushes them back: measured from how fast the ear's root moves over the ground, so it works on foot and in the car, `EAR_WIND` 3 units/s² per unit/s up to `WIND_TOP` 1000, and the spring pulls them towards where gravity and that wind together would hang them (a spring towards straight down won against the push). Measured as the angle of the ear tip from hanging, against the root, along her heading: 28° at a trot, 51° at a gallop, about 68° in the car at full speed. `EAR_DRAG` is 14 (was 4.5): angled back, the ear swung with each gallop stride like a pendulum, from straight back to hanging. Within a frame the root moves to the head's new place step by step over the sub-steps, and the head collision is shifted to match: dragged the whole frame's distance in the first sub-step, the chain took it as speed and the ears flew forward in the car. They follow where she is going, not where her head points: the air lifts each ear out to its side across her direction of travel, and the plane that keeps an ear out of her cheeks only applies beside her face (anywhere during a shake, where it keeps a flung ear from folding over her head). As a plane through the whole side of the head it held the outer ear out in front whenever she turned her head to look at something, worst in the car; checked by galloping with her head held turned 1 radian each way (both ears trail straight back, before one stood out 19 to the side) and driving with it turned. Each piece of an ear is a flat oval; which way its flat faces goes from out of her head at the root to out of her body at the tip (`EAR_FLAT`). Faced with the head all the way down, the ear turned whenever her head turned and looked like a stiff flap, though its tip trailed behind (measured in the car with her head snapping between ahead and 1 radian aside: the tips stayed within a few degrees of straight back). Gusts: the line the wind hangs an ear along wavers up and down and in and out in slow uneven waves (three sines, about 1, 1.7 and 2.9 a second, different for each ear), by up to `GUST` 0.9 radians at the tip at 600 a second, more in the car, nothing when still or in the air. Galloping, the tip's swing over two seconds went from 15 by 17 units to 30 by 34; driving, the tips sway some 15 to 25° to either side. Nothing poses them. In the air (`earCalm` 0 from the frame she leaves the ground, back to 1 over about a quarter second after landing) the flutter is off, her rise and fall carries them fully, and their spring and drag are four times stronger, so they ride along. Before, the flutter grows with how fast the ear moves, and a leap's sudden upward speed flipped the ear tips from hanging to straight above her head in two frames; measured as the ear tip's height in the head's frame through a jump from a stand, a trot and a run (it now lifts from −27 to about −12 and settles).
- **Shake.** `dog.shake` (seconds left, 1.4 s). A twist about her spine that travels from head to tail (`shakeAt(sT, u)` in `src/models.js`: roll at place `u` along her, `sT` seconds in; about 4.8 turns a second, each place 1.9 rad of phase and up to 0.4 s behind the head). The head, bowed a little, rolls 1.35 rad each way, the body skin 0.8 rad (applied per vertex in `bendBody`, so the back marks go round with it), the whole body rocks 0.2 rad over the planted paws, the opposite way to the head (as does the skin) (the same roll goes into the leg joints), the front of the spine swings sideways and the rear after it, and the tail whirls last; paws stay planted, eyes shut; the ears are thrown straight out and up (`fling` in `swingEars`). She shakes after a bonk, every other time she is left standing (the other time is the play bow), and on the studio's `shake` button (K). Moving cancels it.
- **Stopping.** A paw caught in the air when she slows finishes its step at once rather than at the gait's pace.
- The model reports `dog.footfall` when a paw lands (the step sound), `dog.gait` (cycle phase) and `dog.feet` (for tests: `x`, `y`, `u` = progress of the step, 1 when standing, `off` = distance from rest).

- **Pointing at birds.** Standing still with a flock on the ground within 760, she points (`watchBirds` in `src/main.js`) for `POINT_FOR` 3.5 s, then relaxes, and points again `POINT_EVERY` 16 s after she started, or at once when another flock is the nearest. Pointing blocks the wag and the idle bow and shake; without the limit, in town (where a flock is nearly always within sight) she stood pointing for good and never did either. Checked: standing 20 s in town she points, wags, bows, shakes, bows, points.

## People (`makePerson` in `src/models.js`)

- **Look.** Cartoon, hand-animated, no simulation (the simulated trot on Lili's toy-like body read as uncanny). Big heads, rounded shapes. The torso, the hips and each limb are one smooth shape from a height profile (`profileGeo`, `limbGeo`): built from separate ellipsoids, joints showed as balls and the torso as stacked bands, like a wooden mannequin. Children stand about 137 high, adults about 185 and built like the children a size up: rounder and chunkier with a big head (`ADULT`, `CHILD`: bone lengths, shoulders, neck, torso profile; slimmer 205-high adults looked worse than the children). Each look (`LOOKS`) is skin, hair (colour and style: short, long, bun, curls, tails), top (colour, short or long sleeves), bottom (trousers, shorts, or a flared skirt wider than the hips, so the legs do not poke through) and shoes. Faces: eyes that blink, brows, a nose, ears, blush on children, and two mouths, a smile and an open one (shown while wobbling, sitting down hard or jumping clear; ready for a scream).
- **Rig.** Hips (sway, turn) > chest (lean, twist) > neck > head; shoulder (swing forward about z, then out about x) > elbow > hand; hip > knee > ankle (the foot is kept about level). +x is the way they face.
- **Walk.** Driven by the ground covered, a step every 0.55 leg lengths, never by the clock: legs swing ±0.32 rad, a knee folds as its leg swings through, heel down in front and toes off behind, the body rises as the legs pass and dips as a heel lands, hips and shoulders turn against each other, arms swing with the opposite leg, the head stays steady.
- **Standing.** Weight shifts from one leg to the other, now and then a look about. Turning to face something, the feet stay where they are and the chest (0.6 of the turn) and head (the rest) turn; past `STEP_TURN` 0.45 rad they step round in `STEP_T` 0.45 s, one foot then the other, each half turning about the foot that stays down (`offX`, `offY` carry that shift, fading once they walk). Turned as one piece, each foot slid 12 to 15 units over a 1.2 rad turn; now under 4 while down.
- **Poses**: paws up on their chest (rocked back, arms up and out), bumped down to sit, jumping clear of the car, waving (arm up and out to the side), crouched petting her (one hand stroking, the other on a knee, smiling), running scared (longer steps, knees high, leaning in, arms up, mouth open), hands on knees catching their breath, and a glance with a little shrug.
- **Reactions** (`updatePeople` in `src/main.js`). Each person has a nature (`lover`, `meh`, `nervous` in `src/world.js`: warmth 0.7, 0, −0.7, children +0.3). What Lili does adds a manner (`MANNER`): standing calmly within 130 for a second +0.4, paws up (trotting into them) +0.25, a bark within 400 −0.7, a near miss with the car −0.9, jumping or galloping into them −1.2. Warmth plus manner of 0.3 or more is love, −0.35 or less is fright, anything between is indifference; a bark or the car never wins love. Love: they come over if she is more than 75 away (`approach`), crouch and pet her for 2.6 s (`love`), hearts and an "aww", then wave, and do not again for 8 s. Indifference: a glance (`glance`, 1.4 s) and they carry on. Fright: a scream (`A.scream`), they run away from her at 230 (children 200) for 1.8 s, still away if she follows, round anything solid (`scared`), stop with hands on knees for 1.4 s (`breath`), then walk back to where they stood (`return`) or on along their path. Paws up is played first as the wobble, then love or indifference follows; frightened people skip it and run. A crash sits them down first. After any reaction they are left alone for 4 s. While someone pets her she is happy (`dog.happy`: a quicker wag, no pointing or idle bow); petted by a child she is excited (`dog.excited`: the tail going at 21 rad/s and wide, tongue out, rump and chest swinging opposite ways with the wag, a little roll). People keep `PERSON_R` 24 apart from each other.

## The simulated skeleton (`tools/sim/`)

Movement posed by hand did not look like a dog. `tools/sim/dog.py` is a springer spaniel's skeleton for MuJoCo, side on: back, neck, head, tail, and for each leg the real bones (shoulder blade, upper arm, forearm, wrist to paw; thigh, shin, hock to paw), with a dog's joint ranges, about 20 kg spread over the bones, and servos limited to a dog's strength. `tools/sim/jump.py` drives the joints from knots over time and searches (cross-entropy, 16 processes, about 3 minutes) for the leap that reaches the asked height and speed, lands forepaws first, ends standing and costs the least effort. The first guess is rough; the result is whatever the physics allows.

- Each case in `CASES` says what is asked: take-off speed, how far her middle rises, and for the high jump how far she must sink first (`crouch`, 13 cm) and how far she may rear (`tip`). Without `crouch` the search found a high jump with almost no wind-up, which is not how a dog does it.
- `python jump.py stand` (long leap) or `high`; `python jump.py stand 0` replays the saved result. Setup, once, outside the repo: `python3 -m venv ~/sim-venv && ~/sim-venv/bin/pip install mujoco numpy pillow`, then run the tools from `tools/sim` with `~/sim-venv/bin/python` (made with MuJoCo 4.0.1 on Python 3.14).
- Output in `tools/sim/out/`: `<case>.json` (every joint's position 100 times a second, and which paws are down), `<case>.png` (a strip), `<case>.npy` (the knots, to resume a search).
- `tools/sim/view.html` plays a capture in slow motion: `/tools/sim/view.html` on the local server.
- **Gaits.** `tools/sim/gait.py` searches for a steady trot or gallop: `python gait.py trot` or `gallop` (about 5 minutes; a second run resumes from the saved result, `--fresh` starts over, `0` replays). Each joint's target is a repeating curve over the stride (a mean and three harmonics), and the four legs run the same curve at different points of the stride (`lag` in `CASES`: diagonal pairs together for the trot; for the gallop the search may move them). She starts standing at the asked speed and runs 7 strides; the first 3 do not count. The cost asks for: the asked speed, no fall, each stride like the last, her middle kept at standing height and level, each paw on the ground for a share of the stride within `duty`, nothing but paws touching the ground, least effort. One stride (the sixth) is recorded to `out/<case>.json` with `loop: true`. `python export.py` writes them into `src/moves.js` as `GAITS`, measured against the standing skeleton (the first frame of `out/stand.json`), with each paw's time on the ground read from its contacts. The skeleton is flat, so left and right are only names: the trot found had the fore and hind paw of the same name down together, which would be a pace, so the trot case swaps the hind legs' names when it records (`swap`).
  - Results so far (`tools/sim/out/`, play them in `tools/sim/view.html`): `trot` 2.4 m/s, stride 0.36 s (86 cm), diagonal pairs, each paw down 25 to 30% of the stride, her middle rising and falling 0.8 cm (standard deviation): `gait.py trot 150 --bounce=0.008` from `trot_bouncy`, because in the game `trot_bouncy` read as too jumpy. `trot_bouncy` (1.5 cm, `bounce=0.015`) was the one approved in the viewer. `trot_flat` is the first trot (stride 0.30 s, paws down 35%, her middle held level): judged not jumpy enough. The bouncy one came from adding `bounce=0.015` (the cost then asks for that much rise and fall instead of none), `Tmin=0.36` and a lower `duty` range to the trot case, and running `gait.py trot 150` from the flat one. `gallop` 3.8 m/s (her `ZOOM`), stride 0.32 s (1.2 m), clean contacts: `gait.py gallop 150 --v=3.8` from `gallop_fast`. `gallop_fast` 5.5 m/s, stride 0.32 s (1.75 m), a moment in the air; a hock still brushes the ground now and then (9 contacts in 4 strides); it was the game's gallop while her top speed was 640. `gallop_quick` 5.5 m/s, stride 0.25 s, clean contacts, but four strides a second is quicker than a spaniel's.
  - How the gallop was reached: searched directly at 5.5 m/s from a hand guess it never stood up (pitched, scrambling at 3.3 m/s). What worked was going in steps from the trot: `gait.py gallop 150 --from=trot --v=3.0` (the trot's curves with the gallop's leg timing), then `gait.py gallop 120 --v=4.2`, then `gait.py gallop 120 --v=5.5`, each resuming from the one before. That gave `gallop_quick` (the stride sat at the 0.25 s lower limit). `Tmin=0.32` was then added to the gallop case and `gait.py gallop 150` run once more for the longer stride.
  - What went wrong first, and why the cost is as it is: with only speed, repetition and effort asked, the trot came out crouched with the hind paws barely touching (on the ground 11% of the stride). Hence the height term and the `duty` range.
- Not done yet: a running take-off, the landing after touchdown and people are not simulated. The game plays the two leaps in a jump (see *Jump* above) and the two strides when she runs (see *Running*).
